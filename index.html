How to read this: Total EPF = the account's original (enrolled) amount × the negotiation fee % — e.g. $5,000 × 25% = $1,250. It does not depend on the creditor — but the settlement amount does: current debt × the creditor's settlement %, and 20% of that settlement is needed in escrow to start it. Normally 100% of the EPF is taken right after the 1st settlement payment, so "on the file" = the full EPF in month 1. Your what-if keeps the same total EPF and only changes how much is taken in month 1; the rest is still collected later.

Questions to think about on this file

  1. Did the client cancel with only one (or few) settlements, after paying for a long time?
  2. How much EPF was taken in the first month(s) — and was another account ready to settle at that time?
  3. Would the extra cash kept in escrow have reached the 20% needed for the next account sooner?
  4. Was additional funds (AF) requested because EPFs ran past the window? Did that pressure the client?
  5. If the guideline took less EPF up front (e.g. 20–30%), how many months earlier could the next settlement have been reached?
